## Title: The DPDP Act for a growing business: what changes, and when

**In one line:** India's Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 sets rules for any business that handles people's personal data (customers, staff, suppliers), and most of its duties start on **13 May 2027**.

### What it is
- **The Act:** Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023.
- **The Rules:** DPDP Rules, 2025, notified on 13–14 November 2025. The Data Protection Board was set up the same day.
- **Phased start:** Consent Manager rules from 13 November 2026; most business duties (notice, consent, security safeguards, breach intimation, retention) from **13 May 2027**.

### Does it apply to us?
If your business collects personal data digitally (a customer list, staff records, CCTV linked to people, WhatsApp orders), very likely yes. You're a **data fiduciary** for that data. Your IT firm or software vendors who handle it for you are usually **data processors**.

### The five things to start on now
1. **Know what personal data you hold, and where.** Which systems, which shared folders, which phones.
2. **Say why you collect it, and get clear consent** where consent is the basis: plain language, no pre-ticked boxes, easy to withdraw.
3. **Take reasonable security safeguards.** The Act expects them; protecting shared folders and remote access is part of that.
4. **Have a plan for a breach:** who decides, and how you inform the Board and the people affected.
5. **Keep data only as long as you need it,** then delete it.

### Where a network check fits (and doesn't)
A Vedha check shows where personal data may be exposed on your office network, such as a file share anyone can open or a database without a password, and proves when that's fixed. It **doesn't** make you "DPDP compliant"; nothing does that by itself.
